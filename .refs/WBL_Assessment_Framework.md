# Universal Work-Based Learning Assessment Framework
### Philosophy & Design Document — v1

---

## 1. Purpose & Guiding Principle

Traditional hard-skill checklists evaluate technique in isolation. This framework starts from a different premise: **hard skills are the context in which the real object of evaluation — professional soft skills — play out.** A student's technical competence matters, but the program is ultimately trying to develop and assess how a student *works*: how they persist through failure, apply and extend what they know, hold themselves to a standard, and stay curious.

Accordingly, this framework is built around three lenses applied to real work, not three disconnected assessment systems:

1. **Hard Skills** — mastery-based, accumulating into micro-credentials
2. **Gross Output** — the workplace-authentic judgment of finished work
3. **Soft Skills** — evaluated *through* the context of doing the work, not as abstract traits

The framework is designed to generalize across WBL environments with very different content (e.g., Apparel Decoration, Carpentry Job Shop) by keeping the *mechanics* universal while leaving the specific skills, credentials, and QC criteria fully configurable per sector.

---

## 2. Core Organizing Unit: The Work Event

Every assessment in this framework attaches to a **Work Event** — a real, discrete piece of work (an order, job ticket, build, print run). Rather than running three separate assessment processes for the same task, a Work Event is viewed through all three lenses at once, mirroring how work is actually evaluated in industry: a supervisor doesn't separately schedule time to judge your technique and your professionalism — they watch you do the job once.

---

## 3. Two-Phase Model

The framework operates in two phases, with different soft-skill mechanics active in each.

### Phase 1 — Scoped / Introductory (hard-skill-heavy)
Students are building foundational technique through structured, instructor-designed units. There is no prior work history yet to draw on.

- Hard skills are taught and assessed directly.
- Dispositional soft skills (Persistence, Commitment to Excellence, Academic Curiosity) are **active and instructor-scaffolded** — specific units are pre-identified as having natural openings for these skills, narrowing student focus rather than leaving it fully open-ended.
- Transfer soft skills (Application of Previous Knowledge, Extension of Knowledge) are **dormant** — there is not yet a credential history to cite against.

### Transition Trigger
A student exits Phase 1 and enters Phase 2 upon earning the prerequisite micro-credentials required for independent work assignment.

### Phase 2 — Open Jobs (live, unscoped work)
Students are assigned real jobs directly, with variable and unscoped demands.

- Hard-skill assessment continues on every Work Event.
- Dispositional soft skills continue formatively, now self-selected against unscoped work rather than instructor-pre-identified openings.
- Transfer soft skills **activate** and become **formally scored** criteria, since a credential history now exists to cite against.
- Gross Output assessment (QC Spot Checks + Holistic Call) becomes the primary output-quality mechanism.

## 4. The Three Assessment Lenses

### 4.1 Hard Skills & Micro-Credentials

- **Cadence:** Every Work Event, both phases.
- **Evaluator:** Instructor.
- **Scored:** Yes — formal, foundational to the framework.
- **Mechanism:** Mastery-based checklist against a defined, *versioned* skill (skills are versioned rather than edited in place, so a change in scope doesn't corrupt the historical record of what a student was actually assessed against).
- **Credentialing rule:** A skill counts toward a micro-credential only if demonstrated across **multiple** Work Events, under varying conditions, **and** only when demonstrated in service of a Work Event that also clears a minimum Gross Output threshold. Technique performed in isolation, without producing acceptable output, does not count toward mastery. This ties technical competence directly to job-readiness rather than treating them as separable.

### 4.2 Gross Output

Two distinct sub-layers, working together:

**a) QC Spot Check** — narrow, frequent, objective
- **Cadence:** Daily selection across the roster; capped at once per student per calendar week. A rotating queue automatically selects whichever students haven't yet been checked that week, removing the risk of coverage gaps from ad hoc instructor discretion.
- **Multi-week jobs:** Re-enter the rotation and are checked every week the job remains active, building a running QC history over the job's lifespan rather than a single snapshot.
- **Evaluator:** Instructor / simulated "shop foreman" review posture.
- **Content:** Measurable, criterion-based checks appropriate to the sector and job (e.g., a part is in tolerance, an assembly is structurally rigid, an inventory count passes audit). Low judgment burden, fast to execute.
- **Function:** An accumulating in-process evidence trail — not a final verdict.

**b) Holistic Output Call** — broad, infrequent, judgment-based
- **Cadence:** At Work Event completion.
- **Evaluator:** Instructor / shop-foreman-style review.
- **Content:** A four-tier holistic judgment on the finished product: *Not shippable / Rework needed / Meets spec / Exceeds spec.*
- **Function:** The actual verdict on the work, informed by (but not mechanically averaged from) the accumulated QC Spot Check history — the same way a real supervisor uses a process history to inform, but not dictate, a final call.

### 4.3 Soft Skills

The five target soft skills split into two structurally different categories.

#### 4.3.1 Dispositional Skills — Persistence, Commitment to Excellence, Academic Curiosity

- **Status:** Purely formative. **Never scored** as direct assessment criteria.
- **Rationale:** Their value improves a student's process, which in turn should show up organically in improved Gross Output and hard-skill mastery over time. Scoring them directly would corrupt their function as genuine self-noticing rather than performance-for-credit.
- **Mechanism — "Do Now / Exit Slip":**
  - **Do Now (session entry):** Student self-selects one (capped at two) dispositional skill(s) to focus on for the session, ideally with the day's actual work in view.
  - **Exit Slip (session departure):** Student reflects with a *specific cited instance* tied to that day's Work Event — not "I was persistent," but what happened, what the obstacle was, how they responded.
  - **Immutability:** Exit Slip entries are never edited or resubmitted. This preserves the fidelity of the reflection as a genuine in-the-moment artifact.
  - **Instructor verification:** Asynchronous, spot-checked for specificity and plausibility against what the instructor observed in passing — not full independent corroboration of every claim.
  - **Feedback loop:** Delivered forward, via the *next* Do Now — never as a correction to a prior entry.
- **Metacognitive purpose:** The daily forced act of naming and recalling a specific skill is itself a learning mechanism, developing students' capacity to think about their own learning — independent of any assessment value.
- **Confidence gradient (data, not a score):** Each instance sits somewhere between student-claimed, instructor-verified, and (occasionally) instructor-witnessed directly. This gradient should remain visible in the record rather than collapsing into a single "credited" flag.

#### 4.3.2 Transfer Skills — Application of Previous Knowledge, Extension of Knowledge

- **Status:** Dormant in Phase 1; **formally scored** starting in Phase 2.
- **Mechanism:** Citation-based claims checked against the student's own credential record.
  - **Application of Previous Knowledge:** Student cites a specific, previously earned skill/credential and states how it was used on the current job.
  - **Extension of Knowledge:** Student cites a specific new capability developed on the current job — one not part of the formally taught/credentialed skill list yet.
- **Verification:** Semi-self-verifying — the instructor checks whether the cited prior credential actually exists on record, and whether the "new" skill is genuinely novel rather than a restatement of something already credentialed. This is closer to fact-checking than subjective judgment, keeping instructor burden low.

## 5. Cadence & Responsibility Summary

| Lens | Component | Phase 1 | Phase 2 | Cadence | Evaluator | Scored? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hard Skills | Mastery checklist | Active | Active | Every Work Event | Instructor | Yes |
| Gross Output | QC Spot Check | — | Active | Weekly per student (daily rotation) | Instructor / foreman posture | Feeds holistic call |
| Gross Output | Holistic Call | — | Active | At job completion | Instructor / foreman posture | Yes |
| Soft Skills | Persistence, Excellence, Curiosity | Active | Active | Daily (Do Now / Exit Slip) | Student (self) + instructor spot-check | No — formative only |
| Soft Skills | Application of Prior Knowledge | Dormant | Active | Per Work Event | Instructor (record check) | Yes |
| Soft Skills | Extension of Knowledge | Dormant | Active | Per Work Event | Instructor (record check) | Yes |

## 6. Why This Holds Together

- **Hard skills are audited exhaustively** because a micro-credential must rest on a defensible, complete evidence trail.
- **Gross Output is sampled, not universal**, because that mirrors how real supervisory judgment is actually allocated — nobody reviews every unit of output either. The QC/Holistic split further mirrors the real distinction between routine quality audits and a final sign-off judgment.
- **Dispositional soft skills are never scored** because their entire value lies in the act of noticing and reflecting, not in performing for a grade — and their impact is expected to surface indirectly, through improved output and mastery over time.
- **Transfer skills are scored, but only once there is a credential history to cite against**, which is also precisely when Application of Previous Knowledge stops being a null case.
- **The Work Event as the universal organizing unit** is what allows this same structure to extend to any future WBL environment — only the specific hard skills, QC criteria, and credential definitions need to be authored per sector; the assessment mechanics themselves do not change.

## 7. Open Items for Sector-Specific Co-Design

The following remain to be authored collaboratively with instructors for each WBL environment before implementation:

- Full hard-skill lists and versioned skill definitions (Apparel Decoration; Carpentry Job Shop)
- Micro-credential groupings and prerequisite thresholds for Phase 1 → Phase 2 transition
- Sector-specific QC Spot Check criteria (measurable, objective standards per job type)
- Phase 1 unit-by-unit mapping of which dispositional skills have natural openings in which lessons
- Minimum-frequency floor or other safeguards for QC rotation fairness across uneven job durations
