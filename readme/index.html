Background

About the Project

Classical Technology is a set of learning tools built by and for a Career & Technical Education technology program. It started as one thing — a digital version of a daily KenKen "Do Now" — and has grown into a small suite: adaptive KenKen and SAT practice, visual course skill maps, and a system for grading real work students do in the program's school-based enterprise. Everything is tied together by school-account sign-in so progress follows the student.

The KenKen app began because our team was making the puzzles by hand: creating them, converting to PDFs, printing, cutting, and grading 100+ a day — over 6,000 in a single year. After reviewing the results (below), we built this online version to generate and score puzzles automatically and serve them at each student's level.

The KenKen puzzle engine is a fork of the WebKenKen project by msakuta. Special thanks to Sid Challa for helping set up the Google Cloud application.

Project Objectives

  • Puzzles are served based on student ability (rank)

How It Works

Signing In & Adapting to You

  1. You sign in once with your school Google account. That keeps you signed in on this device, and it links your work to you so your progress is saved.
  2. Each practice app looks at how you've been doing recently and picks what to serve next — a puzzle at your level, or a question in a skill you're still building.
  3. Every result is saved with the date. Your teacher sees your scores in the Teacher Portal; you see your own on the My Progress page.
  4. You can also use the practice apps without an account by choosing "Play Locally" — nothing is saved in that mode.

Only accounts on the school's domain can save data. See the Privacy Policy for exactly what is stored.

How KenKen Picks a Puzzle

A puzzle's difficulty comes from two things: how big the grid is and how many cells are filled in for you as hints. Both are set by your rank — a number that rises with your average score. A higher rank means a bigger grid; as you move up within a rank you get fewer hints, so you're solving more of the puzzle yourself before it grows. See Rank Structure below.

Test Prep

SAT & PSAT Practice

The SAT English and SAT Math apps use real practice questions from the College Board's official question bank — the same source as the tests themselves — each tagged with its topic, skill, and difficulty. Depending on your grade, you'll see SAT, PSAT/NMSQT & PSAT 10, or PSAT 8/9 questions.

The practice adapts as you go. It keeps track of your recent accuracy in each skill and difficulty level, and leans toward the areas where you're missing more — a skill you've mastered still shows up now and then, but not as often. You move from Easy to Medium to Hard in a skill only after you've answered enough questions there and kept your accuracy above about 70%. Areas you haven't touched yet start at Easy.

Every question comes with an explanation after you answer, and there's a Report button if a question looks wrong — reported questions are pulled from circulation while they're reviewed.

Course Tools

Skill Maps

Skill Maps are interactive, orbit-style diagrams — one per CTE course — that lay out what the course covers. Each major topic is a "planet," the individual skills under it are "moons," and each is tagged with the career clusters it connects to. They're meant to give students, families, and staff a clear picture of a course's content and where it leads.

Current maps: Graphics & Printing Technology, Computer Aided Manufacturing Technology, and Intro to Robotics. The same list of topics and skills also feeds the micro-credential checklist used in Work-Based Learning.

Program

Work-Based Learning

Students in the program do real work in a school-based enterprise — actual orders, builds, and print jobs. Work-Based Learning is how that work is assessed. Instead of grading technique on a worksheet, it looks at each real job through a few lenses at once:

  • Hard skills — specific techniques, checked off as they're shown across several jobs under different conditions. Enough of them add up to a micro-credential.
  • Quality of the finished work — quick weekly spot checks plus an overall judgment call when a job is done, the way a shop supervisor would review it.
  • Professional habits — persistence, holding to a standard, curiosity, and applying or extending what you've learned before. These are rated through the work itself, not as a separate quiz.

The program runs in two phases. In Phase 1 students build foundational skills through structured units. Once they've earned the micro-credentials needed to work independently, they move to Phase 2 and are assigned open jobs, where the professional-habits ratings start counting more directly.

Micro-credential progress shows up on each student's My Progress page, and teachers manage the whole system from the Teacher Portal.

Methodology

KenKen Scoring

Score = (Unsolved / Guesses + 2.5 × Unsolved / Time) × Grid Size × 10

Unsolved is the number of cells the student had to figure out themselves (total cells minus hint cells). Time is seconds elapsed. The grid size multiplier captures the inherent difficulty of larger puzzles — more number choices per cell and more complex arithmetic — independent of how many cells were pre-filled. Time is weighted 2.5× relative to guess accuracy.

Calculating a Grade

Teachers configure three variables: Max Score, Completion Score (%), and No-Submission Score (%). A student's daily qualifying submissions are counted against the required number per day over the assignment period. Grade = count / required × Max Score, floored at the Completion Score if the requirement is met, or at the No-Submission Score if nothing was submitted. A KenKen submission only counts if the score meets or exceeds the student's all-time average.

Grades

How Practice Becomes a Grade

For the daily practice apps, teachers set a target — how many puzzles or questions count as "done" for a day — along with a full-credit score and a score for days with nothing turned in. Your grade for the assignment is based on how many of those daily targets you hit over the marking period. A KenKen game only counts toward the target if its score is at or above your own running average, so finishing has to mean real effort.

Teachers push these grades into PowerSchool with a companion browser tool (DobbsCore). It reads the totals from this site and creates the matching PowerSchool assignment, so nothing is copied by hand. The same tool posts Work-Based Learning results — micro-credentials, job quality scores, and the professional-habits ratings — each as its own assignment.

You can always see the numbers behind your grade on the My Progress page.
